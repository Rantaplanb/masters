## 2. Significant cybersecurity challenges over the next 5-10 years

One of the most significant cybersecurity challenges of the next decade will be establishing trust in increasingly autonomous and interconnected software ecosystems. AI-enabled agents are beginning to operate across source code, cloud infrastructure, business systems, and sensitive data. They can improve productivity, but they also expand the attack surface: a compromised instruction, dependency, credential, or integration may allow actions to propagate at machine speed. At the same time, attackers will use AI to increase the scale and adaptability of social engineering, vulnerability discovery, and intrusion campaigns.

A closely related challenge is software-supply-chain and cloud concentration risk. Modern organizations depend on large networks of open-source packages, CI/CD platforms, managed services, and third-party identities. A weakness in one trusted component can affect thousands of downstream systems. Technical controls alone will not be sufficient. Organizations will need usable governance: clear accountability, least-privilege access, verifiable provenance, continuous monitoring, incident readiness, and policies that keep pace with both technology and regulation.

In my platform-engineering work, I have seen that controls succeed only when they are embedded into normal engineering workflows and supported by clear ownership. This MSc would help me deepen that practical perspective through cybersecurity governance, privacy and data protection, network security, offensive security, and secure autonomous systems. I want to learn how to evaluate these risks systematically and translate research, regulation, and threat knowledge into security guardrails that engineering teams can adopt at scale.

## 4. Collaboration to solve a professional problem

At Sitecore, I was selected for a cross-functional DevOps Tiger Team responsible for deploying the company's cloud products into the Azure US Central region. The work had to be completed under significant capacity constraints and within a short timeframe; without a successful rollout, fifteen SLA breaches were projected.

My role was to help turn a broad, high-risk objective into executable platform and deployment work. This required close coordination across product teams, cloud and platform engineers, and stakeholders responsible for delivery and reliability. A central challenge was that each product had different dependencies and assumptions, while all teams were competing for limited regional capacity. Optimizing one service in isolation could have created problems elsewhere.

We established a shared view of dependencies, capacity constraints, readiness, and operational risk. I focused on making deployment paths repeatable, identifying blockers early, and communicating exceptions with enough evidence for the team to make quick decisions. When trade-offs were necessary, we prioritized service continuity and validated changes incrementally instead of relying on a single large rollout.

The team completed the deployment in under one month and avoided the projected SLA breaches. The experience reinforced that difficult technical programmes succeed through shared situational awareness and disciplined decision-making, not individual heroics. I learned to communicate uncertainty earlier, make constraints visible, and treat coordination mechanisms as part of the technical solution. These lessons continue to shape how I approach security and resilience work, where fragmented ownership can be as dangerous as a software defect.

## 6. Cybersecurity topic of particular interest

I am particularly interested in the security and governance of autonomous and agentic systems. In my current role, I develop composable AI workflows for activities such as alert triage, ticket analysis, and feature implementation. These systems can reduce response times and remove repetitive work, but they also introduce a new security model: software is no longer only processing information; it may decide which tools to use and perform actions across code repositories, cloud environments, and business systems.

The key challenge is preserving control without eliminating the value of autonomy. Agentic systems need carefully scoped identities, least-privilege tool access, protection from untrusted instructions and data, secure software supply chains, reliable audit trails, and explicit boundaries for human approval. Their behavior also needs to remain explainable enough for organizations to assign responsibility and demonstrate compliance. Traditional application security controls remain necessary, but they are not sufficient when behavior is dynamic and actions can cross several trust boundaries.

During the MSc, I would like to study how zero-trust principles, policy-as-code, behavioral monitoring, threat modeling, and cybersecurity governance can be combined into a coherent control framework for agentic systems. A potential thesis direction would be to design and evaluate an architecture using short-lived scoped credentials, risk-based approval gates, and tamper-evident action records.

After the programme, I would like to apply this work in real engineering platforms and contribute reusable patterns that help organizations adopt autonomous systems responsibly, with security and accountability built in from the beginning.
